01 · The decision you asked about

One profile or five?

Your instinct was "one". Your instinct is right — but only if you understand why mixed feeds usually die, because it is not the reason most people think.

1

One identity, one grade

Every post — myth, car, ad, cartoon — goes through the same darkness, the same title card, the same 1.5s eclipse sting. The subject changes; the studio never does. That is what makes a mixed feed read as range instead of chaos.

2

Pillars, not moods

A fixed weekly ratio does the work an algorithm manager would do. Myth is the magnet, machines are the proof, ads are the money, cartoon is the mascot. The audience learns the rhythm in three weeks.

3

Spin off only a character

The one thing that earns its own account is a character with a daily POV — your 3D cartoon. A topic does not deserve an account; a personality does. Earliest: month 4, and only if the shorts travel on the hub first.

Why there is no "AI" in the name

AI is a tool, not a promise. In three years "AI studio" will sound the way "digital agency" sounds now — true of everyone, differentiating for no one. Worse: the moment a client hears "AI" before "film", they price you as a software user, not a director. Keep AI in the bio, the service page and the sales conversation. Keep it out of the name, the handle and the logo lock-up.

Why not a god's name

Your anchor genre is devotional. A studio named after a deity creates three problems at once: trademark friction (religious names are restricted), audience sensitivity when you use the same name for a car ad, and a ceiling on how dark the work can go. GRAHAN is astronomy, not theology — it can bless a Mahagauri film and sell a sedan in the same week without contradiction.

05 · The host

Rahu — the one who swallows the light

You asked for a named recurring character. Here is the design brief. Everything below exists to solve a real production problem, not just to look cool.

Rahu · always half-lit

Why a silhouette, not a face

AI video still drifts on faces between generations. A hooded, half-lit, rim-lit figure with a serpent motif is the most reusable character design in existence — every render looks intentional because you never see enough of the face to break continuity. You get a mascot, a logo, and a production cheat code in one decision.

His job

He is the narrator who refuses to explain. One line at the top, one line at the end. Never a presenter, never a tour guide. Mystery is the product.

The Swallow

Every single film ends the same way: the light is drawn out of frame into his mouth, the frame goes black, and the GRAHAN mark appears for 1 second. A free, loopable, unmistakable brand device.

Sensitivity guardrail: Rahu is an astrological entity, and some viewers will be protective of it. Rule: he may never be shown as a villain and never as a god. He is a narrator — a shadow that speaks. If you are not comfortable, the alternate name is CHHAYA (છાયા, the shadow): identical design, zero theological friction, and honestly an easier sell to brand clients.
06 · The history

You asked to create a history. Here is the founding myth.

Pin this. Put it on the About page. Read it at the top of every pitch deck. A studio without an origin story is a freelancer with a logo.

In 2025, a director in Vadodara noticed something about every AI video he was being shown.

They were all full of light. Glowing gods. Glowing cars. Glowing everything, all at once, all the time — as if the only thing a new camera could do was turn the brightness up.

Nobody was filming the dark.

So he began with the one image every Indian already knows without being taught: the moon going black. Rahu opening his mouth. The whole country walking out onto their terraces at the same second, looking up at the same sky, holding their breath for something they had seen a hundred times and still could not explain.

That is what a film is supposed to do. Not glow. Make everyone look up at once.

He named the studio after that second — the one where the light disappears and a hundred crore people go quiet together. GRAHAN.

07 · The feed

Architecture: four series, one look

Series names are the cheapest brand system you will ever build. They let one account hold four genres without ever looking confused.

ANDHAAR · EP 01–∞

Your flagship mythology anthology, 45–75 seconds, Hindi voiceover, Gujarati subtitle optional. Start with the stories that are already dark: Rahu swallowing the sun, Kali and the night, Bhairav at the cremation ground, Shumbha–Nishumbha, Matsya and the flood, Yama and Nachiketa.

MACHINES · 01–∞

Automobile spec films with no client. One a month. Rim light, wet asphalt, dust, no badges. This series is your sales team: it is the only thing an ad agency needs to see to forward you to a client.

WORKS · by category

UGC-style sample ads for real D2C categories — but always labelled SPEC, never faked as real campaigns. Format: hook in 1.5s, problem, product, proof, CTA. This is what converts a DM into a ₹15,000 job.

CHHOTE ASUR · 07

3D cartoon shorts about small, petty, hilarious asuras failing at villainy. Cute-dark, no dialogue needed, exportable to any language, and the natural seed of your spin-off account in month 4.

08 · The rollout

90 days, three gates

Gates exist so you stop guessing. If a gate is missed, you change the plan — not the effort.

Days 1–10 · Lock the identity

Nothing gets posted until all of this is true

  • Trademark search on the name (ipindia public search) — free, 20 minutes
  • Handles secured on Instagram, YouTube, X, LinkedIn, Behance — same string everywhere
  • Domain: grahan.studio, fallback .in / .films / getgrahan.com
  • Rahu locked: 3 consistent reference frames you will reuse forever
  • One-page site: 3 films, 4 lines of copy, a contact button. Nothing else.
  • Rate card + invoice template + a business email (not gmail-with-a-number)
  • Nine-post bank finished. Announce only when you have nine.
Days 11–45 · Build the signal

Prove one lane works before touching the others

  • 18 posts on the weekly grid, weighted to ANDHAAR
  • Every post ends on the Swallow sting, no exceptions
  • Comment on 20 mythology/auto pages a day for the first 3 weeks — this is how a new account borrows an audience
  • Gate: one reel crosses 50,000 views, and profile visits exceed 3,000/month. If not, the problem is the first 1.5 seconds — fix hooks, not genres.
Days 46–90 · Convert

Turn attention into invoices

  • Double down on the single best-performing myth. Make EP 04 its sequel, not a new topic.
  • CHHOTE ASUR teaser → if it travels, plan the spin-off for month 4
  • Three outbound pitches a day: ad agencies, car dealerships, D2C brands. Attach a 20-second spec film made for them.
  • HIRE highlight + keyword DM ("FILM") + a 3-question brief form
  • Gate: two paid jobs, at any price. Price is not the point — case studies are.
What to do when a lane dies: give any series eight honest posts before you judge it. If it has not produced a single save or share in eight, kill it and put the hours into the winner. Do not carry four dead series out of loyalty to the plan.

11 · Guardrails

The five things that can end a young studio

1 · Devotional sensitivity

Your anchor is mythology, so you will be held to a higher standard than a meme page. Put the darkness in the world — asuras, storms, scale, night — never in the deities. No "edgy" reimagining of gods, no deity imagery in paid brand work, no AI faces of living gurus. One consultation with a scholar early buys you years of credibility and a shield in the comments.

2 · Fake brand campaigns

Never publish a spot using a real brand's logo as if it were commissioned. Label every one SPEC in the first frame. In India, ASCI guidelines and trademark law both bite here, and one angry brand can erase your client pipeline overnight.

3 · Hiding the AI

Do not hide it — reframe it. Use platform synthetic-content labels, and always say "directed by" before "generated with". Clients forgive tools; they do not forgive being misled. The label costs you nothing and protects you when a video travels.

4 · Naming before filing

Search the trademark before you print a single T-shirt or pay for a logo. File class 41 (entertainment/production) first, then 35 (advertising) when revenue justifies it. Govt fee is modest for an individual/startup filing per class — check the current ipindia fee schedule, it changes.

5 · Chasing every genre

You listed five genres because you can do five. Do not let that become five half-brands. The pillar ratio exists precisely so your ambition has a container. When in doubt, make the myth.

6 · Silent failure

Post the work even when it is not perfect. A studio with 40 published films beats a studio with 4 perfect ones and a folder of ideas. The compounding asset here is volume of finished work, not polish.
